## Part 1 — Sources

Verification vocabulary is defined in the standard, §4.1. **VERIFIED — RECORD
READ** means the abstract was retrieved and read; sample sizes and findings
below are taken from it. **VERIFIED — INDEX ONLY** means bibliographic details
were confirmed against Crossref but the abstract was not read — **no sample
size, effect size or quotation is claimed from those two**.

### S1 — Spancken, Steingrebe & Stein (2021)
*Factors that influence performance in Olympic air-rifle and small-bore
shooting: A systematic review.* PLoS ONE 16(3):e0247353.
DOI [10.1371/journal.pone.0247353](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0247353)
· **VERIFIED — RECORD READ**
Systematic review (PRISMA), 14 studies, 268 participants; small-bore only
4 studies / 23% of participants. Shooting performance is multifactorial.

### S2 — Mononen, Konttinen, Viitasalo & Era (2007)
*Relationships between postural balance, rifle stability and shooting accuracy
among novice rifle shooters.* Scand J Med Sci Sports 17(2):180–185.
PMID 17394480 · **VERIFIED — RECORD READ**
58 novices, 10 m. The balance/accuracy relationship held **only at the
inter-individual level** (r −0.29 to −0.45) — i.e. between athletes, not within
one athlete over time.

### S3 — Mullineaux, Underwood, Shapiro & Hall (2012)
*Real-time biomechanical biofeedback effects on top-level rifle shooters.*
Appl Ergon 43(1):109–114. PMID 21529773 · **VERIFIED — RECORD READ**
n = 5 vs n = 4. The authors state that the actual cause requires further
research.

### S4 — Salmoni, Schmidt & Walter (1984)
*Knowledge of results and motor learning: a review and critical reappraisal.*
Psychol Bull 95(3):355–386. PMID 6399752 · **VERIFIED — RECORD READ**
Origin of the guidance hypothesis: frequent augmented feedback can support
immediate performance while degrading retention.

### S5 — Ihalainen, Kuitunen, Mononen & Linnamo (2016)
*Determinants of elite-level air rifle shooting performance.* Scand J Med Sci
Sports 26(3):266–274. PMID 25850700 · DOI
[10.1111/sms.12440](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.12440) ·
**VERIFIED — RECORD READ**
40 international/national air-rifle shooters, 13 795 shots. Stability of hold,
cleanness of triggering, aiming accuracy and timing of triggering accounted for
**81% of the variance in shooting score**; postural balance **under 1%
directly** (though correlated with holding stability, R = 0.55).
**The keystone source of this register** — see standard §2.1.

### S6 — Era, Konttinen, Mehto, Saarela & Lyytinen (1996)
*Postural stability and skilled performance — a study on top-level and naive
rifle shooters.* J Biomech 29(3):301–306. PMID 8850636 ·
**VERIFIED — RECORD READ**
Posture control over the 7.5 s before the shot. Group separation by skill level.
Comparing each shooter's best 20 vs worst 20 shots, **only the amateurs showed
a notable balance difference**; for top-level shooters a posture-stabilisation
miss was seldom the reason for a poor result.

### S7 — Shea & Morgan (1979)
*Contextual interference effects on the acquisition, retention, and transfer of
a motor skill.* J Exp Psychol Hum Learn Mem 5(2):179–187. DOI
[10.1037/0278-7393.5.2.179](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.5.2.179) ·
**VERIFIED — INDEX ONLY**
The origin of the contextual-interference effect. Cited for existence and
provenance only.

### S8 — Barreiros, Figueiredo & Godinho (2007)
*The contextual interference effect in applied settings.* Eur Phys Educ Rev
13(2):195–208. DOI
[10.1177/1356336X07076876](https://doi.org/10.1177/1356336X07076876) ·
**VERIFIED — RECORD READ**
Review of contextual interference in applied settings: **roughly 60% of applied
studies fail to reproduce the laboratory benefit**. Effect strength depends on
task type, inter-trial interval and feedback.

### S9 — Ammar, Trabelsi, Boujelbane, Boukhris, Glenn, Chtourou & Schöllhorn (2023)
*The myth of contextual interference learning benefit in sports practice: A
systematic review and meta-analysis.* Educ Res Rev 39:100537. DOI
[10.1016/j.edurev.2023.100537](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2023.100537) ·
**VERIFIED — INDEX ONLY**
Cited for existence and title only. **No effect size is claimed from it.**

### S10 — Winstein & Schmidt (1990)
*Reduced frequency of knowledge of results enhances motor skill learning.*
J Exp Psychol Learn Mem Cogn 16(4):677–691. DOI
[10.1037/0278-7393.16.4.677](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.16.4.677) ·
**VERIFIED — RECORD READ**
Reduced (50%) knowledge of results improved retention relative to 100%, despite
poorer performance during acquisition.

### S11 — Guadagnoli & Kohl (2001)
*Knowledge of results for motor learning: relationship between error estimation
and knowledge of results frequency.* J Motor Behav 33(2):217–224.
PMID 11404216 · DOI
[10.1080/00222890109603152](https://doi.org/10.1080/00222890109603152) ·
**VERIFIED — RECORD READ**
64 participants, force-production task, 2 × 2 design (error estimation required
or not × 100% or 20% knowledge of results). **The group required to estimate
its own error and given 100% feedback performed best in retention.**
The direct evidence base for Call & Diagnose.

### M1 — Order statistics of the sample range (mathematical property, not a source)
An extreme-spread measure is an **order statistic**: it is fixed by the two
outermost observations and discards the rest, and its expected value rises with
sample count because a larger sample has more opportunity to contain a distant
pair.

**Correction (2026-07-30).** The first version of this entry quoted the
control-chart constant `d2(n)` — 1.128 (n=2), 2.326 (n=5), 3.078 (n=10),
3.735 (n=20), after Tippett (1925) — and derived an expected ratio of
`d2(20)/d2(5) ≈ 1.61`. **That was misapplied.** `d2` describes the expected
range of a **one-dimensional** normal sample; Tech Aim's group diameter is the
**maximum pairwise distance among two-dimensional points**, a different
statistic with a different distribution. The figure illustrated the direction
of the effect, not its magnitude for this metric, and it is no longer presented
as a derivation anywhere.

**The qualitative property stands** and is what EVID-WM-001 rests on. It is now
demonstrated deterministically instead: two groups built with identical sample
SD at n = 5 and n = 20 differ in extreme spread by more than the 1.25 compact
ratio (`tests/reliability/tst_windmap_dispersion.cpp`, case 5).

**Consequence for Tech Aim:** extreme spread may be displayed, but may not be
compared between groups of different size. See
`../training-lab-wind-map-dispersion.md`.

### Claims that were considered and are NOT supported

- **Shooting-group diagnosis charts** ("vertical string = breathing", "left
  group = trigger") — the available material is commercial coaching pages,
  blogs and forum posts. **Not cited. Not used. Never to be used** as the basis
  of an athlete-facing claim (standard §4).
- **Numeric wind-deflection figures for .22 LR at 50 m** — no primary source
  verified. No number is quoted anywhere in the product or its documents.
- **Peer-reviewed thresholds for interpreting group dispersion in ISSF
  shooting** — none found. Every dispersion threshold in the Training Lab is
  therefore a REASONED PRODUCT RULE, not a standard.

## Part 3 — Defects raised by this audit

### EVID-WM-001 — Wind Map group-width comparison is biased by sample size
**Programme:** Wind Map · **Severity:** HIGH — could produce a wrong
athlete-facing verdict · **Status:** **CORRECTED 2026-07-30** · **Raised:**
2026-07-30

**Correction.** Classification moved from extreme spread to **radial RMS
dispersion**, `sqrt(SUM((x-mx)^2+(y-my)^2)/(n-1))`, which uses every shot. The
1.25 / 1.50 ratios are unchanged; only the metric they apply to. Extreme spread
remains as a descriptive display figure and is no longer compared between
groups. The superseded 40 mm constant is gone from the code; the
elevated-dispersion rule is now radial RMS against 1.5 ring spacings. Every
analysis is stamped `windmap-analytics-v2`. Sixteen deterministic unequal-sample
cases guard it. Full specification: `../training-lab-wind-map-dispersion.md`.

**Remaining:** the 1.25 / 1.50 ratios are still unvalidated product rules and
appear in the coach review pack.

---

**Original report, retained:**

`isWider` / `isCompact` compare extreme spread between two conditions whose shot
counts need only each reach 5. Expected extreme spread grows with n (M1), so a
condition with many more shots than the reference tends to show a larger
diameter with **identical underlying dispersion**. At n = 20 against a reference
of n = 5 the expected ratio (≈ 1.61) already exceeds the 1.50 threshold, so
`WiderUnderCondition` can be produced by sample size alone.

**Candidate remedies** (for a later focused phase; **not** applied during the
audit): compare mean radius instead of extreme spread; or normalise by d2(n);
or require comparable sample sizes before comparing; or downgrade the evidence
level when sizes differ materially. The choice needs Arnold's decision — mean
radius changes the reported number, which is a visible product change.

### EVID-PT-001 — The PT-05 caveat is duplicated in QML instead of read from the model
**Programme:** Position Transition · **Severity:** MEDIUM ·
**Status:** **FIXED 2026-07-30** · **Raised:** 2026-07-30

`PositionTransitionReportView.qml:201` hard-codes the cross-position comparison
caveat as a QML string literal, duplicating the authoritative copy at
`PositionTransitionController.cpp:695`. The two texts are byte-identical today,
but nothing keeps them so — this is exactly the "conclusions rewritten in QML"
failure that standard §6 forbids, on the **single most important sentence in the
programme** (PT-05).

**Fix.** PT-05 and PT-08 are now composed once as
`PositionTransitionController::crossPositionCaveat()` and `reviewDisclaimer()`,
exposed as properties. The report view binds `POSTRANS.crossPositionCaveat` and
holds no copy. The governance checker no longer tolerates a duplicate: it fails
if ANY central caveat text appears as a QML string literal, and separately
requires the report view to bind the central property.

### EVID-GEN-001 — No coach has reviewed any Training Lab rule
**Programme:** all · **Severity:** MEDIUM · **Status:** OPEN

Twenty-eight reasoned product rules drive athlete-facing text and **none has been
reviewed by a qualified coach**. Priority order for review: TB-05 (block
defaults), GP-01 (pattern categories), PT-02 (rhythm labels), PT-06 (the settled
hint), CD-02 (awareness bands), then the Wind Map nine.

### EVID-GEN-002 — Three surfaces are not yet provably one model
**Programme:** all · **Severity:** MEDIUM · **Status:** OPEN

Wind Map composes verdict text in C++ and formats it in QML. Technical Blocks,
Call & Diagnose and Position Transition also compose in C++ — good — but **no
test proves** that the PDF model and the UI model carry the same verdict IDs,
evidence level, limitations and coach-decision text, or that the operator manual
does not restate conclusions in its own words. Tests added by this phase cover
the register and the documents; the cross-surface equality tests are specified
in the standard §6 and remain to be written against the PDF models.

### Out of scope, but found and worth a decision — Coach Report fatigue claims

The **Coach Report** (`src/analytics/`, bridge `COACHREPORT`) is a separate
frozen subsystem, **not a Training Lab programme**, so it is outside this
phase's scope and nothing about it has been changed. It is recorded here because
this audit walked past it and the finding should not be lost.

`CoachDashboardView.qml` and `CoachDetailedView.qml` present **"Fatigue level"**,
a **"Fatigue index"**, a **"Most fatigued" position**, and pattern labels
including `PositionSpecificFatigue` and `FatigueCompensation` — all derived from
shot data. Under the standard, asserting fatigue from impact and timing data
alone would be a prohibited claim (§5, limitation 2).

Mitigating context: the Coach Report is **coach-facing rather than
athlete-facing**, and its labels carry an explicit confidence value.

**No action taken in this phase.** Being coach-facing or frozen does not exempt
it from evidence governance, so it is now recorded as a **DEFERRED EVIDENCE
AUDIT — REQUIRED BEFORE VERSION 1.0** in
`../project/Current_Project_State.md`. Scope when it runs: the fatigue concepts
above, the confidence values attached to them, the recovery analysis, and every
other athlete- or coach-facing conclusion the engine produces.
